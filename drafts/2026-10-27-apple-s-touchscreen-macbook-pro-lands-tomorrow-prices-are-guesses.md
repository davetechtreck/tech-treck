Apple has confirmed exactly one thing about tomorrow: the date. It invited press to a "Pro and tell" experience in Los Angeles at 10 a.m. Pacific on October 28. Everything else you're about to read in the rumor mill, including the first touchscreen Mac laptop, is reported or estimated, not announced.

**What's reportedly coming**

Bloomberg's Mark Gurman says three machines are on deck. The headline act is a redesigned 14-inch and 16-inch [MacBook Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=MacBook+Pro&tag=techtreck02-20) with an OLED touchscreen. One report says it swaps the notch for a Dynamic Island. Oddly, those are expected to run the M5 Pro and M5 Max, the same chips Apple shipped in March. So the display and the chip generation are on separate schedules this time.

Then there's the boring-but-useful pair. An entry 14-inch MacBook Pro gets the M6 chip, which debuted in the Mac mini in August, and so does the 24-inch [iMac](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=iMac&tag=techtreck02-20). MacRumors says no design changes have been rumored for either. The iMac would jump from the M4 straight to the M6, and reports mention new colors, Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6.

**The price question**

This is where I'd be careful. The cheapest MacBook Pro already costs $1,999 after Apple raised it in June, which Macworld ties to supply chain constraints. Several outlets expect the M6 version to stay there. The current M5 Pro and M5 Max models start at $2,499 and $2,999.

The OLED touch models are the real unknown. Estimates disagree: one reported figure is $2,999 for the 14-inch and $3,499 for the 16-inch, while another outlet says $2,800 and $3,300. Gurman reportedly expects a "significantly higher" price. Treat all of it as guesswork until Apple posts a number. The iMac's $1,499 starting price is also speculation.

**My take**

If you need a laptop now and don't care about touch, the M6 14-inch is probably the sensible target, assuming it holds the line at $1,999. It's a chip swap, and nobody should pay extra for a rumor. The iMac is similar: a quiet upgrade, mostly interesting if you're on an older Intel or early M1 machine.

The touchscreen model is the one to be skeptical about. Apple's earlier OLED moves on the iPhone and iPad Pro both raised prices, and one developer-focused write-up points out that macOS has no published touch APIs yet. Paying a premium for a touch layer on an operating system that currently treats your finger like a mouse cursor is a bet on future software.

**What to do**

Wait for the actual price sheet and the first hands-on reviews before you order anything. One report says the hardware ships the same day, but that's also unconfirmed. If you're set on the M6 model, check whether the base storage is 512GB or 1TB, since that detail is still being debated.
