The most useful number on the new [ThermoWorks [RFX Meat](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=RFX+Meat&tag=techtreck02-20) Mini](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=ThermoWorks+RFX+Meat+Mini&tag=techtreck02-20) is 2.36 inches. That's the minimum immersion line, down from 3.15 inches on the full-size probe, and it's the whole reason this thing exists.

The original RFX Meat is 4.7 inches long. Engadget notes that's too long for smaller cuts of meat, poultry and fish. The ThermoWorks RFX Meat Mini is 4.0 inches, which means you can actually get the sensors into a tenderloin or a thin fillet without the probe poking out the other side.

**What you keep**

Nearly everything. The Mini has four internal temperature sensors and an IP69K waterproof rating, so it's dishwasher safe. It can take up to 1,000 degrees Fahrenheit. It uses the same sub-1GHz radio system as the bigger probe, which ThermoWorks pitches as a sturdier link than Bluetooth through smokers and oven walls. The charging block runs on a AAA battery and is rated for over 175 charges.

**What you give up**

Battery life, mostly. The Mini runs up to 48 hours per charge, while the RFX Meat goes past 65 hours. Honestly, that's a non-issue. Forty-eight hours covers a long low-and-slow session at home, and a full charge takes about 20 minutes. You'd have to forget to dock it for days to care.

**The catch is the Gateway**

The Mini is $99, the same as the RFX Meat at full price. It won't work on its own, though. Because of the radio frequency it uses, you need an [RFX Gateway](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=RFX+Gateway&tag=techtreck02-20) to connect it. I couldn't confirm a standalone Gateway price in my sources, so check ThermoWorks before you budget. If you're starting from zero, you're buying into a system, not a single probe.

There's a small deal on now. ThermoWorks is taking 20 percent off the Mini when you buy either an RFX Gateway or an RFX Meat starter kit. Twenty percent off $99 is $79.20, which matches the sale price on the ThermoWorks product page.

**Who should buy it**

If you already own an RFX Gateway and cook a lot of small stuff, like pork tenderloin, chicken pieces or fish, the Mini is an easy add. It's a sensible second probe next to a full-size one. ThermoWorks says the Mini is compatible with its existing RFX setup, and the full-size probe is still there for roasts and briskets.

If you only ever smoke big cuts, skip it. The longer RFX Meat gets you more battery for the same price, and you gain nothing from the shorter body.

If you're new to wireless probes, don't buy the Mini first. Get the starter kit that includes the Gateway, then decide whether your cooking actually needs a short probe. Many people will find the full-size one works fine for most meals.

Before you order, check whether the 20 percent offer is still running, and make sure your cart includes a Gateway or a kit that has one.
