---
title: Samsung Galaxy Buds Pro Gets FDA Clearance for Hearing Aid Features
date: 2026-09-13
tags: samsung, earbuds, wearables
snippet: Samsung's upcoming Galaxy Buds Pro models will include FDA-cleared hearing aid technology by end of 2026.
---

Samsung just cleared a major regulatory hurdle that could change what we expect from premium earbuds. The company's Galaxy Buds Pro line has gained FDA clearance for hearing aid technology, and Samsung says the feature will arrive in two Pro models before the end of the year.

This matters because it puts Samsung in direct competition with Apple's AirPods Pro 2, which added FDA-authorized hearing aid functionality in a software update last year. Apple made a lot of noise about democratizing hearing health, but the reality is that most people who need mild-to-moderate hearing assistance still don't use traditional hearing aids due to cost and stigma. Putting clinical-grade hearing features into earbuds people already want to buy is smart product strategy.

The timing is interesting. Samsung is clearly playing catch-up here, but the company has advantages Apple doesn't: broader price points, more model variations, and deep integration with Android. If Samsung can match Apple's implementation quality and maybe undercut on price with a non-Pro model down the line, this could actually reach more people who need it.

We don't know yet which two Pro models Samsung is targeting or what the exact feature set looks like. Will it be a full hearing aid mode with personalized sound profiles, or something more limited? Can it do real-time sound amplification in conversation mode? Those details matter, because Apple's implementation is legitimately good enough that audiologists have recommended it for some patients.

The broader pattern here is worth noting: consumer earbuds are becoming genuine health devices. First it was fitness tracking via heart rate sensors, then fall detection, now hearing assistance. Samsung and Apple are both betting that the next generation of premium earbuds needs to justify $200-plus price tags with features that go beyond just better noise cancellation and longer battery life.

If Samsung nails the execution, this could be the feature that convinces some people to switch from AirPods Pro. More realistically, it removes one of Apple's few remaining exclusive health features and keeps Samsung competitive in a market where differentiation is increasingly hard to find. We'll know more when Samsung announces which models are getting the feature and what it actually does in practice.
