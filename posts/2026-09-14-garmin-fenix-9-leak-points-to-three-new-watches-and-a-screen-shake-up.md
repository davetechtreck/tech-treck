Garmin's next flagship fitness watch lineup is apparently taking shape, and if the latest leaks hold up, the Fenix 9 series will arrive with three distinct models and what sounds like a meaningful shift in display technology.

The Fenix lineup has long been Garmin's answer to serious outdoor athletes and endurance junkies who need multi-day battery life, bulletproof GPS tracking, and enough sensors to make a laboratory jealous. The current Fenix 8 generation brought AMOLED screens to the party alongside the traditional memory-in-pixel (MIP) displays that defined earlier models, giving buyers a choice between vivid color or marathon battery runtime.

Now it looks like Garmin is doubling down on that screen strategy for the Fenix 9. The leaked details suggest we're getting three watches in the lineup, and the most interesting wrinkle is how Garmin plans to handle display tech across those models. The company has spent years perfecting its transflective MIP screens, which are readable in direct sunlight and sip power like nothing else in the smartwatch world. But AMOLED has become table stakes for premium wearables, and Garmin clearly knows it can't ignore that expectation.

What's still unclear is whether Garmin will segment the three models by screen type, size, or feature set, or some combination of all three. The Fenix 8 launched with 43mm, 47mm, and 51mm case sizes, plus the AMOLED versus MIP screen choice, which created a dizzying matrix of SKUs. Simplifying that would be welcome. So would better solar charging performance on the AMOLED models, which took a meaningful hit compared to their MIP siblings.

Garmin has been on a tear lately, and the Fenix series remains its halo product for the outdoor and endurance crowd. The company doesn't typically leak details ahead of launch, so the fact that this information is circulating suggests we might be closer to an announcement than expected. If Garmin can nail the display tech balance, keep the battery life competitive, and avoid turning the lineup into a spreadsheet of confusing options, the Fenix 9 could be another solid step forward.

But let's be honest: Garmin's real competition isn't other outdoor watches. It's convincing casual buyers that a $700-plus fitness tracker is worth it when an Apple Watch or Galaxy Watch will do most of what they need for half the price. Three models and better screens won't solve that problem, but they'll certainly keep the Garmin faithful happy.
