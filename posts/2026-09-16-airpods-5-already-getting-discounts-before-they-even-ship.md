One week. That's how long Apple's [AirPods 5](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=AirPods+5&tag=techtreck02-20) have been on the market before Amazon decided to start discounting them. The earbuds were announced at Apple's September 9 event alongside the iPhone 18 Pro lineup and the foldable iPhone Ultra, and they're not even shipping yet. But Amazon is already offering them at $124.99, down from what Apple is calling a surprisingly affordable launch price to begin with.

This is unusual timing for a first-party discount on a brand-new Apple product. Typically, Amazon waits at least a few weeks after launch before undercutting Apple's pricing, and even then it's usually by $10 or $20. But here we are, a week after the announcement and before the official release date, with a discount already live.

The AirPods 5 represent Apple's latest refresh to its entry-level wireless earbuds. Based on the announcement, they're available in both base and active noise cancellation variants, marking a shift in how Apple is positioning its AirPods lineup. The company appears to be moving away from the numbered progression that previously distinguished feature sets, instead offering choice at the same generation level.

What makes this discount particularly notable is the context: Apple launched these at what reports described as an already competitive price point. For Amazon to immediately undercut that suggests either fierce competition in the wireless earbuds space or perhaps softer-than-expected preorder numbers. Either way, consumers benefit.

The timing also raises questions about Apple's pricing strategy under new CEO John Ternus, who took the reins earlier this year. The company positioned the September event as a "huge launch," but between the controversial decision to skip the base iPhone 18 until early 2027 and now immediate discounting on the AirPods, it's not clear the premium pricing strategy is holding as firmly as it once did.

If you've been waiting to upgrade your earbuds, this is a solid entry point. The AirPods 5 should deliver the usual Apple ecosystem integration, decent sound quality, and reliable connectivity. And at $125, they're competing directly with mid-tier options from Sony, Jabra, and others that historically offered better value than Apple's premium-priced buds.

Just don't expect this discount to last long once the earbuds actually start shipping. Amazon's early price cuts tend to disappear once supply stabilizes and demand becomes clearer.
