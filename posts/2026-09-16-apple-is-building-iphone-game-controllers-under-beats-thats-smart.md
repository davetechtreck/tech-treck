---
title: Apple Is Building iPhone Game Controllers Under Beats. That's Smart.
date: 2026-09-16
tags: gaming, apple, beats, controllers
snippet: Apple has been quietly working on two Beats-branded iPhone game controllers for over a year, targeting the mobile gaming accessory market.
---

Apple is reportedly developing two gaming controllers designed for the iPhone, and in an uncharacteristically savvy move, they're planning to launch them under Beats instead of slapping an Apple logo on them and charging twice as much.

According to Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, the project has been in the works for over a year, with Beats executives overseeing development. Code references in macOS 26.7 revealed two controller models with identifiers T6502 and T1057, both featuring standard gamepad fare: dual thumbsticks, D-pad, shoulder buttons, triggers, and haptic motors. The higher-end T1057 model appears to add Bluetooth support alongside USB-C, plus an accelerometer and gyroscope for motion controls.

The Beats branding is the interesting part. Apple already sells game controllers in its stores from Backbone, SteelSeries, and Sony, but those partnerships mean Apple only gets a retail cut. Building its own controllers makes sense as mobile gaming becomes more viable on increasingly powerful iPhones. What doesn't make sense is pricing a controller at typical Apple premiums when a PlayStation DualSense already works perfectly fine with iOS.

That's where Beats comes in. By tucking these controllers under the Beats brand, Apple can target more aggressive price points without cheapening the mainline Apple product aesthetic. Beats has always been the brand where Apple experiments with louder designs, broader demographics, and lower margins. It's why Beats headphones come in actual colors and don't cost $549.

The timing matters, too. Razer just launched its ultra-compact Prio controller at $100, and mobile gaming hardware is having a moment as console prices climb and smartphone chips close the performance gap. Apple has been pushing Metal as a credible gaming platform and porting titles like Resident Evil and Death Stranding to iPhone and Mac. Selling your own controllers is the logical next step, but only if you can price them competitively.

No word yet on release dates or exact pricing, and as with any code-based leaks, the project could shift or get canceled entirely. But if Apple follows through, these could become the first genuinely interesting gaming accessory the company has built in years. As long as they don't cost more than the phone itself.
