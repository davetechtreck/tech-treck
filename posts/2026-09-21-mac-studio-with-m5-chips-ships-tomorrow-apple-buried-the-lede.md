Tomorrow, September 22, Apple will ship the new [Mac Studio](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Mac+Studio&tag=techtreck02-20) with M5 Max and M5 Ultra processors. If that launch date sounds familiar, it's because it's the same day the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max hit stores. Apple announced the Mac Studio last month with barely a whisper, and now it's arriving in one of the busiest product launches the company has had in years.

The new Mac Studio starts at $2,499 and comes configured with either the M5 Max or M5 Ultra chip. For anyone keeping count, that's Apple's desktop workstation slotting into a launch window that also includes two flagship iPhones, a foldable iPhone Ultra that shipped last week, two new Apple Watch models, AirPods 5, and potentially a handful of smart home products.

It's a strange bit of timing. The Mac Studio is a serious machine aimed at video editors, developers, and creative professionals who need desktop-class power in a compact form factor. Those buyers don't typically overlap with the iPhone upgrade crowd, and burying a workstation launch in the middle of Apple's annual phone frenzy feels like a missed opportunity to let the hardware breathe.

The M5 Max and M5 Ultra represent meaningful performance upgrades over the M2-generation chips in the previous Mac Studio, which hasn't been refreshed since early 2023. The new silicon should deliver substantial gains in rendering, compiling, and anything that leans hard on GPU compute. But the conversation around this machine has been drowned out by the iPhone spectacle and the iPhone Ultra's creaseless foldable display.

Apple clearly wanted to pack September with as many launches as possible. The company is also shipping the new Mac mini on the same day, creating a double desktop launch that would normally warrant its own event or at least a focused PR push. Instead, both machines are getting lost in the noise.

For anyone in the market for a Mac Studio, tomorrow is the day. Just don't expect much fanfare when it arrives. Apple's already moved on to the next thing.
