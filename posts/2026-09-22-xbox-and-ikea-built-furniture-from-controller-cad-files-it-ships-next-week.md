IKEA and Xbox just confirmed something genuinely weird: a nine-piece furniture collection launching October 1 that's built directly from the engineering files of an actual Xbox controller. Not inspired by. Built from the same CAD data that defines the thumbsticks and D-pad you've been mashing for 25 years.

The [YXSTABY](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=YXSTABY&tag=techtreck02-20) collection includes a thumbstick stool that rocks when you sit on it, a D-pad cushion, a lounge chair with a mesh controller holder built into the side, and a mirrored display case designed specifically for showing off controllers and collectibles. Prices run from $18 for the D-pad platter to $180 for the side table on casters. Buy all nine pieces and you're out about $575.

What separates this from the usual gamer furniture playbook is restraint. IKEA's 2021 ASUS ROG collaboration leaned hard into black geometric aggression. This one barely registers as gaming furniture at all unless you've spent enough time with an Xbox controller to recognize the exact curve of a thumbstick translated into a 35-pound stool. The palette is neutral grays and whites with occasional flashes of Xbox green. The shapes are subtle enough that a non-gamer walking into your living room would probably just think you have eclectic taste.

That's the point, apparently. Xbox's Carl Ledbetter said the team designed every piece to sit naturally alongside furniture people already own, not dominate a dedicated gaming cave. IKEA's Philip Dilé framed it as solving the problem of gaming setups that need to work in shared spaces where not everyone cares about your hobby.

The thumbstick stool is the obvious conversation piece here. It tilts slightly when you sit on it, mimicking the movement of an actual analog stick, and comes in black or bright green for $50. The lounge chair at $128 includes a dedicated pouch that holds a controller at arm's reach without looking like a tactical gear attachment. The TV stand on casters holds two monitors, clips for headphones and controllers, and costs $155.

The collection goes on sale in US stores October 1 and online November 30. UK and Canadian launch is October 1 across the board. IKEA says availability is while stocks last, which is a polite way of saying this might sell out fast if the internet decides thumbstick stools are the new Billy bookcase.

It's a strange collaboration that somehow makes sense. Xbox gets to mark its 25th anniversary with something more tangible than a limited-edition controller shell. IKEA gets to prove it can do gaming furniture that doesn't scream "gaming furniture." And you get to explain to guests why you're sitting on a giant green joystick.
