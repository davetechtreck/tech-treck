Google is pushing the boundaries of AI-powered smartphone features in a way that feels both impressive and deeply uncomfortable. The company just announced that select Gemini Advanced subscribers who own a [Pixel 11](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Pixel+11&tag=techtreck02-20) will soon be able to hand off their phone calls to Google's AI chatbot.

The feature does exactly what it sounds like: when you need to make a call — say, to reschedule a dentist appointment or check restaurant hours — Gemini can dial, navigate phone trees, speak to whoever picks up, and relay the information back to you. It's an extension of the call screening features Google has been building for years, but this time the AI isn't just listening. It's talking.

From a pure convenience standpoint, the appeal is obvious. Phone calls remain one of the last bastions of genuine friction in daily life, especially for administrative tasks nobody wants to do. Letting AI handle the hold music and repetitive questions sounds great.

But there's something unsettling about outsourcing human conversation to a chatbot without the person on the other end necessarily knowing they're not talking to you. Google hasn't detailed how transparent the feature will be during calls, which matters a lot. Will businesses know they're speaking to an AI? Will your dentist's receptionist realize she's rescheduling with software?

The Pixel 11 exclusivity also highlights an emerging pattern: Google is using its Gemini subscription tier and its own hardware to gate its most ambitious AI experiments. That makes sense from a controlled rollout perspective, but it does mean you'll need both the phone and the $20-per-month Gemini Advanced plan to even try this.

It's not clear yet when the feature will actually roll out beyond the initial test group. Google has been steadily adding AI call capabilities to Pixel phones over the past few years — first with screening, then with hold-for-me, and now full conversation delegation. Each step inches closer to a world where your phone is less a communication device and more a personal assistant that occasionally impersonates you.

Whether that's the future we want is a different question than whether it's the future we're getting.
