Apple has a new CEO, and for the first time in over a decade, it's not the supply chain guy calling the shots.

John Ternus officially took over as Apple's chief executive on September 1, 2026, replacing Tim Cook, who moved into an executive chairman role. The transition happened quietly, just days before Apple's biggest product launch of the year—the September 9 event that introduced the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone Duo foldable, Apple Watch Series 12, and AirPods 5.

Ternus isn't a household name, but he's been Apple's senior vice president of hardware engineering since 2021, overseeing the teams that build iPhones, Macs, and the entire M-series chip transition. Before that, he led Mac engineering and played a key role in developing the company's custom silicon strategy. In other words, this is the guy who actually makes the products, not just the guy who gets them built on time and under budget.

That's a meaningful shift. Cook, who ran Apple for 13 years after Steve Jobs, came from operations. His superpower was making Apple the most efficient manufacturing machine in consumer tech. Ternus, by contrast, is a product person. He's an engineer who spent decades in the details of thermal design, chip architecture, and industrial engineering. He knows why the hinge on a foldable iPhone matters, not just how many units shipped in Q3.

The timing is telling. Apple is in the middle of its biggest hardware reinvention in years. The iPhone just went foldable. Macs are getting touchscreens for the first time. Siri is being rebuilt from scratch with on-device AI. These aren't iterative spec bumps—they're bets that require deep product judgment, not just operational excellence.

And Ternus is taking over at a moment when competitors like Google and Samsung are moving faster on AI integration and form-factor experimentation. Apple needs someone who can make hard calls on what ships and what doesn't, not just someone who can optimize the supply chain.

The executive chairman role for Cook isn't ceremonial—he'll still oversee board relations and major partnerships—but the day-to-day product decisions now sit with someone who's been in the room where those products were designed. That could mean faster iteration, weirder experiments, or just a different kind of Apple.

Either way, the hardware guy is in charge now. And the first products on his watch just shipped.
