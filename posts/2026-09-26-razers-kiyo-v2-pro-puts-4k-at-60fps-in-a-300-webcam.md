Razer just released the [Kiyo V2 Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Kiyo+V2+Pro&tag=techtreck02-20), a $300 webcam that captures 4K video at 60 frames per second. That spec alone makes it notable in a market where most consumer webcams still drop to 1080p or 1440p the moment you push past 30fps.

The heart of the upgrade is Sony's 8.3-megapixel STARVIS 2 sensor, the same back-illuminated chip Elgato uses in its latest 4K webcam. Razer paired it with an f/1.9 lens, which is wider than what most webcams offer and should improve low-light performance while creating stronger background blur.

Beyond the hardware, the Kiyo V2 Pro adds AI-powered auto-framing that keeps you centered in the frame using digital pan, tilt, and zoom. It's the same trick Apple uses in FaceTime calls on recent iPhones, and it works well enough when you're moving around during a stream or presentation. There's also one-click image enhancement that automatically adjusts exposure, white balance, and noise reduction based on your environment.

For those who want manual control, Razer Synapse software lets you tweak ISO, shutter speed, and other settings directly. The webcam includes both a monitor clamp and a universal tripod mount, plus a privacy shutter styled to look like an actual camera shutter mechanism.

The Kiyo V2 Pro also supports Ultra-High Dynamic Range, which should retain more detail in highlights and shadows. The field of view is 86 degrees, narrower than the 93 degrees on the standard Kiyo V2, but Razer says the tighter frame reduces edge distortion at 4K.

At $300, it sits firmly in premium webcam territory. The price reflects a bet that streamers, remote workers, and creators would rather spend on a dedicated webcam with high specs than rig up a smartphone or compact camera as a stand-in. Whether 60fps at 4K and better low-light performance justify that cost depends entirely on how often you're actually on camera and how much motion blur bothers you.

The Kiyo V2 Pro is available now through Razer's site and other retailers. No word yet on UK pricing or release dates outside the US.
