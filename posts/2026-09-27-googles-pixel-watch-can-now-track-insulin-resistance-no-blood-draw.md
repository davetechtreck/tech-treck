Google started rolling out its Health Guardian features this week, and the headline capability is genuinely ambitious: insulin resistance trend tracking that requires no blood sample. The suite also includes blood pressure trends and sleep breathing quality metrics, all now available for free on [Pixel Watch 3](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Pixel+Watch+3&tag=techtreck02-20), 4, and 5.

The insulin resistance feature analyzes weeks of physiological data collected passively by the watch to detect metabolic changes that could signal a shift toward type 2 diabetes. Google says it trained the model on more than a trillion minutes of data from 5 million opted-in users. You wear the watch, and a month later, you get a summary. No finger pricks, no lab visit.

The blood pressure trend feature is equally hands-off. Unlike Samsung's Galaxy Watch implementation, which requires monthly calibration with an actual cuff, Google's system works entirely in the background. It uses a model called WavesFM to analyze pulse shape, speed, and motion data collected over at least five consecutive days per week. The watch won't give you an on-demand reading the way Samsung's can, but it will flag persistent upward trends in a monthly report and suggest you follow up with a real monitor or your doctor.

Google is careful to frame all of this as wellness monitoring, not diagnostics. The company explicitly says these features should not be used to diagnose hypertension, diabetes, or sleep apnea, or to adjust medication. They're trend spotters, not medical devices.

Still, the technical achievement here is notable. Estimating insulin resistance from wrist-based sensors is the kind of thing that sounds like vaporware until it isn't. If Google's models hold up in real-world use, this could become the new baseline expectation for what a smartwatch should do.

The features are rolling out now inside the Google Health app, with the first trend reports expected in early October. [Pixel Watch 4](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Pixel+Watch+4&tag=techtreck02-20) and 5 owners in select European countries also get Breathing Emergency Detection, which can call emergency services if the watch detects a severe, sustained drop in blood oxygen and the wearer becomes unresponsive.

[Fitbit Air](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Fitbit+Air&tag=techtreck02-20) users will eventually get access to blood pressure and insulin resistance trends later this year, but only with a Google Health Premium subscription, which costs around $9 per month as a standalone or is included with Google AI Pro at roughly $22 per month.

For now, this is a Pixel Watch perk. And for once, it's one that actually sounds useful.
