Apple announced its first foldable iPhone at its September event, and then promptly went quiet on the things people actually want to know. The [iPhone Duo](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=iPhone+Duo&tag=techtreck02-20) ships October 23, with pre-orders opening October 16, starting at $1,999. That part's clear. What's not clear is almost everything else.

The device uses two screens connected by a continuous frame, creating what Apple calls a fluid transition between open and closed states. When fully open, it's the thinnest iPhone Apple's ever made. The two halves share the same aspect ratio across both displays, which is a different approach than Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold series with its narrow outer screen.

But Apple skipped over crucial durability details. There's no mention of crease visibility, no water resistance rating announced, and no information about whether the displays use ultra-thin glass or plastic. These aren't minor spec-sheet details—they're the make-or-break questions that determine whether a $1,999 foldable lives in your pocket or a drawer after six months.

The other glaring omission: camera specs. Apple showed the Duo alongside the [iPhone 18 Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=iPhone+18+Pro&tag=techtreck02-20), which got a detailed camera walkthrough, but said almost nothing about what lenses the foldable actually has. One report notes the lack of a telephoto lens, which would be a strange choice at this price point, but Apple hasn't confirmed that either way.

The matte screen finish is confirmed, which should help with glare but might feel odd if you're used to the glossy panels on every other iPhone. And unlike the iPhone 18 Pro models that launched September 18, the Duo is coming a full month later—enough time for early reviews to surface, or for production issues to leak.

Apple's silence on durability is the most telling part. Every other manufacturer launching foldables leads with hinge cycles, screen protection layers, and warranty terms because those are the obvious concerns. Apple skipped all of it. That's either supreme confidence or a sign that the answers aren't ready yet.

The October 16 pre-order date gives Apple three more weeks to fill in the blanks. Until then, you're buying a $1,999 question mark with the thinnest chassis Apple's ever built and almost no concrete details about how long it'll stay that way.
