Sony released a Sand Pink colorway for the [WH-1000XM6](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=WH-1000XM6&tag=techtreck02-20) over-ear headphones in February, joining the existing Black, Platinum Silver, and Midnight Blue options. It costs $459.99, ten dollars more than the other colors for reasons Sony hasn't bothered to explain.

The new shade is nice enough—a muted peachy tone that splits the difference between millennial pink and actual sand—but the more interesting story is buried underneath the press photos. The WH-1000XM6, which launched last May at $449.99, has quietly become the default premium headphone recommendation despite a $50 price bump over its predecessor.

That's partly because Sony brought back the folding hinge that the XM5 controversially ditched, making the XM6 both more portable and more practical than the model it replaced. But it's also because the competition hasn't kept pace. The [Sonos Ace Ultra](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Sonos+Ace+Ultra&tag=techtreck02-20) costs the same and sounds excellent, but it's locked into Sonos's ecosystem in ways that matter if you don't already own Sonos speakers. Bose's QuietComfort Ultra Headphones run $449 in the UK but feel iterative rather than essential.

The XM6 improved noise cancellation over an already class-leading predecessor, added Bluetooth 5.3 support, and kept the 30-hour battery life that makes daily charging unnecessary. Sony also upgraded to a QN3 processor that's seven times faster than the chip in the XM5, though most of that speed goes toward better ANC algorithms rather than features you'll consciously notice.

The Sand Pink model doesn't change any of that. It's the same headphones in a different finish, available now through Sony's website and major retailers. The premium over the standard colors is annoying but small enough that it probably won't matter to anyone who's already decided they want pink headphones.

What does matter is that Sony's managed to raise the price, eliminate color parity, and still produce the safest recommendation in the category. That's the kind of market position that lets you charge an extra ten bucks for paint.
