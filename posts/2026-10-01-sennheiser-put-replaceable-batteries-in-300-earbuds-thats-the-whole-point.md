---
title: Sennheiser Put Replaceable Batteries in $300 Earbuds. That's the Whole Point.
date: 2026-10-01
tags: earbuds, sennheiser, audio, repairability
snippet: The Momentum True Wireless 5 solves wireless earbuds' biggest problem: batteries that die long before anything else breaks.
---

Sennheiser just did something no other major earbud maker has done: made the batteries replaceable. The Momentum True Wireless 5 earbuds, which started shipping in early September for $299.95, let you swap out the lithium-ion cells in both the earbuds and the charging case using a Phillips PH000 screwdriver. Sennheiser even throws the tool in the box.

This matters because battery degradation is what kills premium earbuds. The drivers, Bluetooth chipsets, and microphones can outlast the tiny cells powering them by years, but once capacity fades below a usable threshold, you're stuck buying a new pair or living with 90-minute listening sessions. Sennheiser is betting that buyers willing to spend $300 on earbuds also want them to last longer than 18 months.

The company claims up to 12 hours per charge with ANC off, six hours with it on, and 40 hours total with the case. That matches or beats most flagship competitors at launch, but the real value proposition kicks in two or three years down the road when you can order replacement cells instead of shopping for a new pair.

There's a catch, though: Sennheiser hasn't announced replacement battery pricing, SKUs, or where you'll actually buy them. The company also hasn't published a step-by-step repair guide or clarified whether DIY battery swaps void the one-year warranty. So while the hardware supports user replacement, the full ecosystem isn't there yet.

The rest of the spec sheet is flagship-grade but unsurprising. You get Bluetooth 6.0, aptX Lossless, Snapdragon Sound, 7mm TrueResponse drivers made in Ireland, adaptive ANC, Dolby Atmos with head tracking, and IP54 splash resistance. Five colors: Graphite, Cream, Copper, Denim, and Lavender. Qi wireless charging on the case. An 8-minute quick charge buys you an hour of playback.

None of that is new. What's new is Sennheiser treating a $300 product like something that should last. Marshall and Sennheiser's own over-ear Momentum 5 Wireless already have swappable batteries, but this is the first time a major brand has brought the concept to true wireless earbuds at scale. If replacement batteries actually show up for sale at reasonable prices, it changes the cost-per-year math in a way that matters more than another codec or another decibel of ANC.

The Momentum True Wireless 5 is available now through Sennheiser's site and select retailers. Whether it's the start of a repairability trend or a one-off experiment depends entirely on how many people are willing to pay for longevity they can't yet fully verify.
