Neurable wants you to know when your brain is tired before you do. The company just launched [Neurable One](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Neurable+One&tag=techtreck02-20), its third pair of EEG-equipped headphones, this time built entirely in-house instead of bolted onto someone else's hardware. It's available today for $499.

That's the same price Neurable charged for last year's MW75 Neuro LT, the lighter follow-up to its original $699 collaboration with Master & Dynamic. But the Neurable One isn't just a rebranded sequel. The company designed these from scratch, which means it finally controls the whole stack: 12 fabric EEG sensors sewn into the ear pads, 40mm beryllium drivers, six-microphone ANC, and the algorithms that try to make sense of your brainwaves while you work, rest, or doom-scroll.

The pitch is familiar. The headphones track your focus in real time, then nudge you to take a break when your attention starts to crater. But Neurable One adds two new metrics: capacity, which estimates how much more you can focus before burnout hits, and alpha peak frequency, a long-term brain health marker that drops as you age. The second one requires weeks of passive data collection, so it's less a daily dashboard and more of a longitudinal commitment.

Battery life sits at 22 hours with EEG off, 12 hours with brain tracking active. That's competitive with the [Sony WH-1000XM6](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Sony+WH-1000XM6&tag=techtreck02-20) and [Bose QuietComfort Ultra](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Bose+QuietComfort+Ultra&tag=techtreck02-20), which both cost around the same but lack the neural monitoring party trick. Whether dry EEG sensors in headphones can deliver clinically useful data is still an open question. Researchers have noted that dry electrode systems are inherently noisy, and real-time feedback based on that signal risks overpromising.

But Neurable isn't selling medical devices. It's selling the promise that you can optimize your workday by listening to your brain instead of just your calendar. At $499, you're paying premium-headphone prices for that experiment. The audio hardware is solid enough to justify the cost on its own, but the EEG is the reason you'd pick these over anything else at this price. If you're curious whether your brain has been trying to tell you something, Neurable One is now shipping in the US.
