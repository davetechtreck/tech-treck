Microsoft's refreshed [Surface Pro 12-inch (2nd Edition)](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Surface+Pro+12-inch+%282nd+Edition%29&tag=techtreck02-20) arrives October 13 starting at $1,149.99—a significant jump from the previous model, which launched at $849 (a 35% increase). Even comparing apples to apples, the base 16GB/256GB config now costs $100 more than the outgoing generation.

What changed? The new Surface Pro 12 switches to Qualcomm's Snapdragon X2 Plus processor, paired with up to 24GB of RAM and optional 5G connectivity. The display got a bump to 500 nits of peak brightness—25% brighter than before—and the battery is rated for an extra hour of runtime. The tablet also claims a "30% more efficient" power design.

But here's the actual move: Microsoft made the Surface Pro 12 fanless. No moving parts, no thermal noise. That's a clean engineering trade-off, and on paper it sounds good for a thin, light device. The tablet weighs just 1.5 pounds and measures 0.30 inches thick. For a detachable Windows machine meant for sketching and light work, silent operation matters.

The spec sheet tells the rest of the story. There's still just one LCD option (no OLED), the display maxes out at 90Hz, and battery life on active web usage drops to 13 hours from the previous generation's 12 hours—modest gains for the premium you're paying. You get 16GB or 24GB of RAM standard now (the 8GB option is gone), and storage starts at 256GB.

Microsoft has been aggressive with Surface pricing this year, and that's hitting harder on the smaller models. If you're comparing to the original Pro 12, this is basically a tax for Snapdragon support and a brighter screen. The fanless design is real engineering, but it's buried in a pricing story that feels more about market positioning than feature value. Those looking for tablet-laptop hybrids at this price point will notice the jump.
