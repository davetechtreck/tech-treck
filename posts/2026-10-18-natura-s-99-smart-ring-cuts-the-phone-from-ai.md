[Natura Interface](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Natura+Interface&tag=techtreck02-20) launches October 8 at $99—a price that undercuts Samsung's Galaxy Ring by four-to-one. The play is straightforward: ship a screenless interface for AI agents when everyone else is bundling another screen into the wearable stack.

You press a button on the ring, speak, and release. The Interface sends your request to an AI agent of your choosing—Claude, ChatGPT, Meta's Muse, Grok—and returns the result. No phone required. The stainless steel ring sits on your index finger and connects to more than 140 agents through NatureOS, Natura's software ecosystem.

The hardware calculus is sharp. Natura's costs to manufacture the Interface track closely with Oura's production overhead, yet Oura retails at $350. Natura is absorbing the difference: early adopters pay $99, which drops to free for three to six months after purchase. After that free window, subscription pricing kicks in at $9 per month. The annual cost rises beyond the launch hardware price, but still undercuts Galaxy Ring's flat $400 outlay.

Battery life sits between six and twelve days depending on usage, with a full charge taking roughly 100 minutes. The ring also doubles as a health tracker—the device monitors heart rate, sleep, activity, and skin temperature—so users get passive monitoring even when they're not commanding agents. Natura runs this tracking on a custom low-power chip that operates independently via Bluetooth when disconnected from a phone.

Preorders open next month with shipments targeted for December or January. There's a catch worth noting: Natura's official product page currently lists Interface as a waitlist offering, meaning final pricing and delivery details remain unconfirmed. The $99 figure comes from TechCrunch's initial reporting, not from a locked checkout.

The bigger question isn't price or battery life. It's whether a single physical button can become a genuinely useful interface for AI agents in daily life. Founder Carlo Edoardo Ferraris built the Interface after concluding that people need 24/7 access to AI without reaching for their phone. The ring tests that premise. If it works, the form factor matters less than the motion: press, speak, release. If it doesn't, you're wearing another device that promises to reduce screen time while actually just adding complexity.

No independent reviews exist yet. Natura has not announced a broader retail rollout beyond the initial launch window. Battery claims, agent response times, and health tracking accuracy all remain unconfirmed by third parties. For a $99 wearable with subscription strings attached, that's the real risk—not the price tag.
