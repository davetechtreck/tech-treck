The headline spec on Technics' new SL-1210MKX is a pitch setting of ±50%. That's a huge swing for a turntable, and it tells you who this thing is for. Not you, unless you're a scratch DJ or you really want to be one.

**What Technics announced**

The SL-1210MKX is the new top model in the SL-1200 line. It sits above the SL-1210MK7. Per MusicTech, it has a heavier chassis, a ±4% pitch range that's a first for the series, and the new ±50% option. It was shown publicly at Pacific in Amsterdam during the 30th anniversary edition of Amsterdam Dance Event.

Technics says it built the deck with working DJs and sound engineers. The target is touring, club and festival DJs, plus scratch and performance specialists. The company pitches it at high-pressure settings with powerful sound systems, deep bass and long hours.

**Price and timing**

It goes on sale in January 2027 at £1,299, or €1,499. I couldn't find a US price or a US date, so treat both as not confirmed. The launch also lines up with the 55th anniversary of the SL-1200 series, which Technics dates to 1972.

**How it compares**

For a rough yardstick, Technics' US store lists the [Technics SL-1200MK7](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Technics+SL-1200MK7&tag=techtreck02-20) at $1,299.99. A retailer listing for that model describes pitch faders at ±8% and ±16%. That's a different currency and a different market, so don't read the numbers as a like-for-like price comparison. Still, the UK price of the MKX looks like it's in the same ballpark as the MK7's US sticker. Technics hasn't said what the heavier chassis weighs, so I can't tell you how much bulkier it is than the MK7's listed 9.6 kg.

**Who should care**

If you play records at home, the MK7 already covers the basics, and a ±50% pitch slider won't change your listening. Most people never push a deck far from its set speed. Extreme pitch ranges are a performance tool. Same goes for the weight. A heavier body is aimed at bass-heavy rooms where vibration is a real problem, not a living room shelf.

If you're a working DJ, or a bedroom scratch DJ who wants the pro deck, this is a more interesting upgrade. Technics clearly built it for the booth rather than for the audiophile crowd.

**What to do**

Don't pre-order anything yet. There's no US pricing, and a deck that costs this much deserves a proper look at reviews first. If you need a turntable now, the MK7 is still listed and isn't going anywhere. If you're a DJ, wait for the US announcement and for early impressions of how the new pitch range feels in practice.
